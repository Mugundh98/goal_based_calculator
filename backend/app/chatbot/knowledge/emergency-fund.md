# Emergency fund

## Why an emergency fund comes first

An emergency fund is money kept aside for unexpected expenses or a loss of income, such as a medical bill, an urgent repair or a job loss. Without it, an emergency forces the sale of long-term investments at a bad time or borrowing at high interest. Financial planners usually advise building it before investing for other goals.

## How much

A common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses, including loan EMIs. People with variable income, a single income in the family or dependants should keep more, often six to twelve months. Adequate health and term insurance reduces how much is needed.

## Where to keep it

The money must be safe and available within a day or two, so returns come second. A savings account or sweep-in fixed deposit gives instant access; liquid funds and overnight funds usually earn a little more and can be redeemed within one business day (many fund houses allow instant redemption of a small amount). Equity funds are unsuitable because their value can fall sharply exactly when the money is needed.

## In GoalWise

An emergency fund can be added as a goal. GoalWise always recommends 100% short-term debt for it, whatever the risk profile. The risk questionnaire also counts how many months of expenses the user's savings cover, because a good cushion raises the capacity to take risk elsewhere.
