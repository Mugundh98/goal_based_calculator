# SEBI mutual fund categories

## Why categories exist

In October 2017 SEBI required every fund house to classify its schemes into defined categories, with at most one scheme per category (apart from index funds, ETFs and sectoral funds). This makes funds of the same category comparable: a large-cap fund of one fund house tries to do the same thing as a large-cap fund of another.

## Equity categories

Companies are ranked by market capitalisation: the top 100 are large caps, numbers 101 to 250 are mid caps, and the rest are small caps. A large-cap fund must keep at least 80% in large caps, a mid-cap fund at least 65% in mid caps and a small-cap fund at least 65% in small caps. A large & mid-cap fund holds at least 35% in each. A multi-cap fund holds at least 25% each in large, mid and small caps; a flexi-cap fund holds at least 65% in equity of any size. Focused funds hold at most 30 stocks; value and contra funds follow those styles; sectoral and thematic funds concentrate on one sector or theme. ELSS funds are tax-saving equity funds with a three-year lock-in.

## Debt categories

Debt funds are grouped mainly by how long their bonds have until maturity or by their interest-rate sensitivity (duration): overnight (one day), liquid (up to 91 days), money market (up to one year), ultra-short, low and short duration, and medium and long duration. Others are grouped by the kind of borrower: corporate bond funds (mostly the highest-rated companies), banking & PSU funds, gilt funds (government securities) and credit risk funds (lower-rated bonds, higher risk). Dynamic bond funds change their duration with their view of interest rates.

## Hybrid and other categories

Hybrid funds mix equity and debt: conservative hybrid (mostly debt), balanced advantage or dynamic asset allocation (equity share changes with valuations), aggressive hybrid (65–80% equity), multi-asset (at least three asset classes) and arbitrage funds (low-risk equity arbitrage, taxed like equity). Index funds and ETFs track an index, and fund-of-funds invest in other funds.
