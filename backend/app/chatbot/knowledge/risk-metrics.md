# Measuring a fund's risk

## Volatility

Volatility is the standard deviation of a fund's returns, usually annualised. It says how widely returns swing around their average. GoalWise computes it from daily NAVs over the last three years. Typical equity funds show 12–20% a year, liquid funds well below 1%.

## Sharpe ratio

The Sharpe ratio is the return earned above a risk-free rate, divided by volatility: (return − risk-free rate) ÷ volatility. It answers "how much extra return per unit of risk?". GoalWise uses a risk-free rate of 6% a year and the last three years of data. Higher is better, and the ratio is meaningful only when comparing funds of the same category.

## Sortino ratio

The Sortino ratio is like the Sharpe ratio but divides by downside deviation, which counts only the returns below the target. It does not penalise a fund for rising sharply, only for falling.

## Maximum drawdown

The maximum drawdown is the largest fall from a peak to a later low, as a percentage. It shows the worst loss an investor who bought at the top would have seen before the fund recovered. Equity funds fell by 30–40% in March 2020; a goal that needs money soon should not be exposed to such falls.

## Rolling returns

Rolling returns measure the return over every possible window of a fixed length, for example every one-year period in the fund's history. Their average, minimum, maximum and the share of windows with a positive return describe how consistent a fund has been, far better than a single trailing return that depends on the start and end dates.
