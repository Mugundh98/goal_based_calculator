# Costs of investing in mutual funds

## Expense ratio

The expense ratio is charged every day inside the NAV, so investors never pay it separately; the published returns are already after it. It is the largest ongoing cost, and a lower expense ratio is one of the few things that reliably improves future returns. Direct plans have lower expense ratios than regular plans of the same fund.

## Exit load

An exit load is a charge on redemptions made soon after investing, deducted from the redemption amount. It discourages short-term trading that would hurt long-term investors. Many equity funds charge 1% if units are redeemed within one year; liquid funds have a small graded exit load for redemptions within seven days; many debt and index funds have none. Each scheme's exit load is stated in its scheme information document and can change for new purchases. Every SIP instalment has its own exit-load period.

## Stamp duty

A stamp duty of 0.005% applies to purchases (since 1 July 2020), including each SIP instalment and switches into a fund.

## Taxes

Capital-gains tax on redemptions and tax on IDCW payouts are a cost too, which is why rebalancing by redirecting new money is often cheaper than selling.

## Costs in GoalWise

GoalWise includes stamp duty when it works out units for a purchase. Fund returns and metrics are computed from NAVs and are therefore after expense ratios. Exit loads and taxes are not modelled; the rebalancing advice reminds users that redeeming can cost both.
