# NAV, units and how purchases are priced

## Net asset value

The net asset value (NAV) of a mutual fund scheme is the value of one unit: the market value of everything the scheme owns, minus its liabilities and expenses, divided by the number of units. Fund houses publish NAVs every business day, and AMFI collects them in a daily file. A high or low NAV says nothing about whether a fund is expensive or cheap; what matters is how the NAV changes.

## Units and allotment

When you invest, the amount (after any stamp duty) is divided by the applicable NAV to give the units allotted, usually to three decimal places. When you redeem, units are sold at the applicable NAV. Orders placed before the cut-off time on a business day get that day's NAV; later orders or holidays get the next business day's NAV.

## Stamp duty

Since 1 July 2020, a stamp duty of 0.005% of the purchase amount is deducted before units are allotted. On ₹10,000 that is 50 paise. GoalWise includes it when it works out units for a purchase.

## Face-value changes

Occasionally a fund changes the face value of its units, for example from ₹10 to ₹1,000 for some liquid funds. The NAV then jumps by a power of ten overnight while each investor's money stays the same, because the number of units changes in the opposite direction. GoalWise detects such jumps and restates older NAVs and units in today's units, so returns and charts are not distorted.
