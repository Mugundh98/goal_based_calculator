# Inflation and real returns

## What inflation does to goals

Inflation is the rise in the general price level. At 6% a year, something costing ₹10 lakh today costs about ₹17.9 lakh in 10 years and ₹32 lakh in 20 years. Every GoalWise goal is entered in today's rupees and inflated to its target year, so the amount to save is what the goal will actually cost then.

## How inflation is measured in India

The Consumer Price Index (CPI), published monthly by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MOSPI), tracks the prices of a basket of goods and services bought by households; GoalWise uses the All India, combined, general index with base year 2012. The Reserve Bank of India's target is 4% CPI inflation with a tolerance band of 2% to 6%.

## Real return

The real return is what an investment earns after inflation: (1 + nominal return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1. A fixed deposit paying 7% when inflation is 6% has a real return of only about 0.9%, before tax. Long-term goals need investments whose real return is clearly positive, which is why equity plays a large role for distant goals.

## Education and medical inflation

Some costs rise faster than general CPI. Higher-education and healthcare costs have often risen by 8–10% a year, so it is sensible to use a higher inflation rate for such goals; GoalWise lets each goal have its own inflation rate.

## GoalWise's inflation forecast

GoalWise forecasts CPI inflation for the next 12 months with a seasonal ARIMA model and shows an 80% range. The forecast is a guide for setting assumptions, not a prediction to rely on.
