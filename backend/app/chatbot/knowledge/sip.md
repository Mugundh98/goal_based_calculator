# Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)

## What a SIP is

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals, usually every month, on a chosen date. Each instalment buys units at that day's NAV, so the investor builds a holding gradually instead of investing a lump sum at one moment. A SIP is a way of paying in, not a type of fund: the same fund can be bought by SIP or as a lump sum.

## Rupee cost averaging

Because the amount is fixed, a SIP buys more units when the NAV is low and fewer when it is high. Over time the average cost per unit ends up below the average NAV over the same dates. This does not guarantee a profit, but it removes the need to guess the right day to invest and softens the effect of buying everything just before a fall.

## Step-up SIP

A step-up (top-up) SIP raises the instalment every year by a fixed percentage, for example 10%, usually in line with salary increases. Starting with a smaller SIP and stepping it up can reach the same target as a larger flat SIP. GoalWise lets every goal have a yearly step-up rate and works out the starting SIP needed with it.

## Discipline and duration

The benefit of a SIP comes mostly from regularity and time. Stopping SIPs after a market fall locks in the lower prices without letting later purchases benefit from the recovery. Equity SIPs are best suited to goals at least five to seven years away; for nearer goals, debt funds are more suitable.
