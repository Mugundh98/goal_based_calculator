# Index funds and ETFs

## Passive investing

An index fund buys the stocks of a market index, such as the Nifty 50 or the Nifty Midcap 150, in the same proportions, and aims to match the index's return rather than beat it. Because there is no stock picking, expense ratios are low, often below 0.3% a year for direct plans of large index funds.

## Exchange-traded funds

An exchange-traded fund (ETF) also tracks an index, but its units trade on a stock exchange during market hours like shares. Buying ETFs needs a demat and trading account, and the price paid can differ slightly from the NAV. Index funds are bought and sold with the fund house at the day's NAV and allow SIPs, which many investors find simpler.

## Tracking error

The tracking error measures how closely a fund follows its index. Costs, cash held for redemptions and the timing of trades make the fund's return slightly lower than the index's. A good index fund has a low expense ratio and a low tracking error.

## Active versus passive

Over long periods many actively managed large-cap funds have found it hard to beat their index after costs, which is why index funds are a popular core holding for large caps. In mid and small caps, active funds have had more success. GoalWise's fund model does not rank index funds against each other, because they track different indices by design.
