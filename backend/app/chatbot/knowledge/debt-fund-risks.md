# Risks in debt funds

## Debt funds are not fixed deposits

Debt mutual funds invest in bonds, government securities and money-market instruments. Their NAV changes every day, returns are not guaranteed and the money is not insured like a bank deposit. They are usually far less volatile than equity funds, but they carry two main risks.

## Interest-rate risk and duration

When interest rates rise, the prices of existing bonds fall, because new bonds pay more. The longer a bond's remaining life, the larger the fall. Duration measures this sensitivity: a fund with a modified duration of 5 years loses about 5% of its value if interest rates rise by one percentage point (and gains about as much if they fall). Liquid and money-market funds have very short durations and barely move; gilt and long-duration funds can swing several percent in a year.

## Credit risk

A borrower may fail to pay interest or repay the bond (default), or its credit rating may be cut, which lowers the bond's price. Funds that hold the highest-rated bonds (AAA, sovereign) carry little credit risk; credit risk funds deliberately hold lower-rated bonds for extra yield and have suffered sharp losses when issuers defaulted.

## Matching debt funds to goals

For money needed within a year, liquid, overnight or money-market funds suit best. For goals a few years away, short-duration, corporate bond or banking & PSU funds are common choices. GoalWise puts goals that are close, and emergency funds, into short-term debt for this reason.
