# Risk profile: tolerance and capacity

## Two different questions

Risk tolerance is how much ups and downs an investor is willing to accept emotionally: would they sell in panic if their portfolio fell 20%? Risk capacity is how much risk they can afford financially, given their age, income stability, dependants, savings and loans. Someone may be comfortable with risk but have little capacity (for example, a single earner with large EMIs), or the other way round.

## How GoalWise scores it

GoalWise asks seven questions about attitude to risk to get a tolerance score out of 100, and computes a capacity score out of 100 from the financial profile: age, how much of the income is left after expenses and EMIs, the emergency cushion, dependants and employment type. The final score is the lower of the two, following standard suitability practice: someone eager to take risk but unable to afford losses should not get an aggressive portfolio, and someone who can afford risk but would panic should not either. The result is one of three categories: conservative, moderate or aggressive.

## How the category is used

The category sets the starting equity share for long-term goals (30%, 60% or 85%), the return assumed in goal projections when the user does not set one, and how strongly the optimiser avoids risk when splitting a goal's money. Retaking the questionnaire after a major life change (marriage, a child, a new job, retirement nearing) keeps the recommendations appropriate.
