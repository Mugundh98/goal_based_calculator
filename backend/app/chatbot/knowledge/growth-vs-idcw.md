# Growth and IDCW options

## Growth option

In the growth option, any income and gains stay invested in the scheme and are reflected in a rising NAV. The investor receives money only when redeeming units. This suits long-term goals because returns keep compounding.

## IDCW option

IDCW stands for Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal; SEBI renamed the old "dividend" option to IDCW from April 2021 to make clear that the payout comes out of the investor's own capital. When the scheme declares a distribution, the NAV falls by the amount paid. The payout can be received as cash (IDCW payout) or used to buy more units (IDCW reinvestment).

## Which to choose

An IDCW payout is not extra return: the NAV drops by the same amount. Payouts are taxed as income in the year they are received, and their amount and timing are decided by the fund house, not the investor. For goal-based investing the growth option is usually more suitable; investors who need regular income can instead use a systematic withdrawal plan (SWP) from the growth option, which lets them choose the amount and the dates.
