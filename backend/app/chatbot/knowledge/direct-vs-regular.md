# Direct and regular plans, and the expense ratio

## Two plans of the same fund

Since January 2013, every mutual fund scheme in India offers a direct plan and a regular plan. Both invest in exactly the same portfolio and are managed by the same fund manager. The regular plan pays a commission to the distributor who sold it; the direct plan, bought straight from the fund house or through a direct platform, pays none. The two plans therefore have different NAVs.

## Expense ratio

The expense ratio (total expense ratio, TER) is the yearly cost of running the scheme, expressed as a percentage of the assets and deducted from the NAV every day. It covers management fees, administration and, in regular plans, the distributor's commission. SEBI sets maximum limits that fall as a scheme grows larger.

## Why the difference matters

A regular plan's expense ratio is typically 0.5 to 1.5 percentage points a year higher than the direct plan's. Over long periods this compounds: on a 20-year SIP, a 1% yearly difference can reduce the final value by roughly a fifth. Investors who want advice may still prefer a regular plan or a fee-only adviser; investors who choose funds themselves usually prefer direct plans.
