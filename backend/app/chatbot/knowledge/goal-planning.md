# How GoalWise plans a goal

## Goals in today's rupees

A goal is entered as what it would cost today, with a target year. GoalWise inflates that cost to the target year at the goal's inflation rate (6% a year by default). For example, ₹20 lakh today in 10 years at 6% inflation becomes about ₹35.8 lakh.

## Assumed return

Unless the user sets a return, GoalWise assumes one from the risk profile: 8% a year for conservative, 10% for moderate (also used without a risk profile) and 12% for aggressive investors. For goals less than three years away it assumes 7%, because money needed soon should not be in equity.

## Projection and required SIP

GoalWise grows the savings already set aside for the goal and the current monthly SIP (invested at the start of each month, with an optional yearly step-up) at the assumed return to the target year. If the projected value falls short of the inflated target, it works out the starting monthly SIP that would reach it, rounded up to the next rupee. A goal is on track when the projection covers the target.

## Effective monthly rate

The yearly return is converted to an equivalent monthly rate, (1 + annual rate)^(1/12) − 1, so that twelve months of compounding give exactly the yearly return. Using a simple twelfth of the yearly rate would overstate growth slightly.

## From plan to reality

The projection uses one fixed return. The goal's simulation shows the range of outcomes with realistic market swings, the recommendation shows how to invest the money, and the portfolio tracker compares the real investments with the plan once transactions are recorded.
