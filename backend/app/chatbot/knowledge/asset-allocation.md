# Asset allocation and diversification

## Asset allocation

Asset allocation is how a goal's money is split between asset classes such as equity, debt and gold. It drives most of the variation in a portfolio's returns and risk, more than the choice of individual funds. Equity offers higher long-term returns with large short-term swings; debt is steadier with lower returns.

## Diversification

Assets that do not move together reduce a portfolio's swings without reducing its expected return proportionally. Large-cap and mid/small-cap equity are highly correlated (they tend to fall together), while bonds often behave differently from equity, so a mix of equity and debt is far less volatile than equity alone.

## Glide path

A glide path reduces risk as a goal approaches, because there is less time to recover from a fall. GoalWise starts from an equity share set by the risk profile (30% for conservative, 60% for moderate, 85% for aggressive investors) for goals ten or more years away, and reduces it in a straight line to zero equity one year before the goal. Mid and small caps are phased out within three years of the goal, and short-term debt takes over the debt part as the goal nears.

## Mean-variance optimisation and the efficient frontier

Harry Markowitz showed that for each level of risk there is a portfolio with the highest expected return; together these portfolios form the efficient frontier. GoalWise splits each goal's equity and debt between four parts (large caps, mid & small caps, longer-term bonds and short-term debt) by maximising expected return minus a penalty for risk, using assumed returns (12.5%, 13.5%, 7.5% and 6.5% a year) and risks measured from Indian fund data. The recommendation page shows the frontier and where the recommended mix sits on it.
