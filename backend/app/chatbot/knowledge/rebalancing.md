# Rebalancing and portfolio drift

## Drift

A portfolio set at 60% equity and 40% debt does not stay there. If equity rises faster than debt, equity may become 70% of the money a few years later, and the portfolio carries more risk than intended. This change is called drift. As a goal nears, the safe equity share also falls, so the target itself moves.

## Rebalancing

Rebalancing brings the mix back to its target: selling some of what has grown too large and buying what has fallen behind. It enforces "buy low, sell high" and keeps risk under control. Common approaches are calendar rebalancing (once a year) and threshold rebalancing (whenever a part drifts beyond a band).

## How GoalWise decides

GoalWise compares each goal's holdings with its latest recommendation. When the equity share, or any part of the mix, is more than 5 percentage points away from the recommended share, it raises a rebalancing alert and shows the amounts to move to get back to the target. A recommendation made when the goal had a different number of years left, or more than a year ago, should be refreshed.

## Rebalancing without selling

Redeeming units can cost capital-gains tax and exit loads. A cheaper way to correct drift is to direct new SIP money, or a bonus or lump sum, into the parts that are underweight until the mix is back on target. Selling makes sense when drift is large or the goal is close.
