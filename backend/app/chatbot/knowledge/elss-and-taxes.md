# ELSS and how mutual funds are taxed (general guide)

## A caution first

Tax rules for mutual funds in India have changed several times, including in 2023 and 2024, and they depend on the investor's situation. This article explains the ideas; always check the rules in force for the year of the sale, or ask a tax professional. GoalWise does not include taxes in its calculations.

## Capital gains

When units are redeemed for more than they cost, the profit is a capital gain. Gains are short-term or long-term depending on how long the units were held, and the holding period and rates differ between equity-oriented funds (at least 65% in Indian equity) and other funds. Units are treated as sold in the order they were bought (first in, first out), so each SIP instalment has its own holding period.

## Equity-oriented funds

As announced in the Union Budget of July 2024, gains on equity-oriented funds held for more than 12 months are long-term and taxed at 12.5% on the part of the year's long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh; gains on units held for 12 months or less are taxed at 20%. Check whether these rates still apply.

## Debt funds

For debt funds bought on or after 1 April 2023, gains are taxed at the investor's income-tax slab rate regardless of the holding period, so they no longer get the older long-term treatment with indexation.

## ELSS

Equity Linked Savings Schemes are equity funds with a lock-in of three years from each investment (each SIP instalment is locked for three years from its own date). Under the old tax regime, investments in ELSS qualify for the deduction under section 80C, up to the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit shared with PPF, EPF, life insurance premiums and others. The new tax regime does not allow this deduction.

## IDCW payouts

Distributions under the IDCW option are added to the investor's income and taxed at their slab rate in the year they are received.
